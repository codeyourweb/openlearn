# Trademark policy

The OpenLearn name and logo identify this project and its official distribution.
The licence (AGPL-3.0-only) grants rights over the code; it grants nothing over
the name, which is what this document covers.

## What you may do without asking

- Use the name to refer to the project, in articles, courses, talks, reviews and
  comparisons.
- Say that your instance "runs OpenLearn" or is "based on OpenLearn", as long as
  it is true and not presented as official.
- Keep the name in the source code, the documentation and the interface of an
  unmodified copy.
- Distribute an unmodified copy under the name, with a link to this repository.

## What requires permission

- Using the name or the logo as the name of your company, product or hosted
  service.
- Using the logo in a way that suggests endorsement, partnership or official
  status.
- Registering a domain, a social account or a trademark that contains
  "OpenLearn".

## Forks

A modified version distributed to others, or offered as a service, must carry a
different name and logo. The AGPL already requires you to publish your changes;
it does not allow you to present them as the official project. A short mention
such as "based on OpenLearn" is welcome and sufficient.

## The Open University

The Open University (UK) operates a free-learning site also called OpenLearn.
This project is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to The Open
University in any way: the code, the maintainers and the hosting are unrelated.
The project's domain carries a qualifier (`openlearnproject.org`) so the two are
not confused. Should The Open University object to the name, it will be changed
rather than argued over.

## Reporting misuse

Open an issue or contact the maintainer. Most cases are a misunderstanding and a
sentence in a README settles them.
